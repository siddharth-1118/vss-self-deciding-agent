# Convergence Report — Was VSS Under-Trained?

**Question.** The plain classifier led VSS in all 35 value-test cells, and VSS's
synthetic validation loss was still falling at the 8-epoch cap. Is the gap
under-training, bad optimization, a bad objective, or the architecture?

**Answer: A + B — under-training *and* an optimization/schedule defect. Not C.**
Both were real, both are fixed, and after fixing them the accuracy gap on the
synthetic task **disappears**. What remains in VSS's favour is step efficiency,
selective prediction, and inference latency.

The single most important finding is that the earlier "plain wins 35/35" result
was a **measurement artifact of three stacked defects**, not a property of the
architecture: a training-set-contaminated evaluation split, a warmup that
consumed 75% of VSS's schedule, and step budgets that handed the plain baseline
8× more gradient signal. `docs/convergence_audit.md` documents the audit that
found them.

All numbers below come from `benchmarks/convergence/tables.md` (generated by
`analyze.py` from `benchmarks/convergence/runs/*.json`) and from the benchmark
result JSONs. Nothing is hand-entered.

---

## 1. Protocol

Fixed before any new result was inspected (`sweep.py` constants, audit §3):

| | |
|---|---|
| Selection metric | validation loss, row-weighted; **test never used for any choice** |
| Learning rates screened | 3e-4 and 1e-3, both systems, same list |
| Early stopping | patience 3, min improvement 5e-3, min 4 epochs |
| Step budget | 400 optimizer steps (synthetic), schedule = cosine over that budget |
| Splits | train / validation (selection) / calibration (thresholds) / test |
| Seeds | **1** (see §7 — this is the study's weakest point) |
| Hardware | 6 physical cores, CPU only, fp32, torch 2.14.0+cpu |

Both systems share one schedule implementation (`lr_lambda`,
`effective_warmup`), one warmup cap, one stopping rule, and one validation slice.
The plain baseline was given the same LR screen and the same early stopping it
was missing before.

**Three budgets are reported, because they answer different questions.**

| protocol | VSS | plain | what it equalises |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **equal optimizer steps** | 400 | 403 | update count — but VSS carries 8 questions per update |
| **equal data presentations** | 400 steps × 8 q = 102,400 | 2,200 steps | (state, question) pairs seen — the like-for-like data protocol |
| **equal wall-clock** | 2,953 s | 4,056 s | CPU time |

## 2. Did VSS reach a defensible convergence point? — Yes

VSS, synthetic, lr 3e-4, 400 steps, 16 epochs, **early-stopped**:

| epoch | train | eval | choice acc | noul acc | score MAE | ECE | lr | grad norm | param update |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 5.3255 | 1.9043 | 0.4850 | 0.486 | 2.102 | 0.056 | 1.95e-04 | 1.415 | 7.10e-05 |
| 4 | 3.1094 | 1.1552 | 0.7600 | 0.838 | 0.949 | 0.078 | 2.61e-04 | 2.426 | 1.28e-04 |
| 8 | 1.0931 | 0.4041 | 0.8675 | 0.954 | 0.247 | 0.028 | 1.43e-04 | 1.505 | 6.61e-05 |
| 11 | 0.8050 | 0.3393 | 0.8550 | 0.954 | 0.179 | 0.041 | 5.36e-05 | 1.314 | 1.88e-05 |
| **12** | 0.7812 | **0.3323** | 0.8650 | 0.955 | 0.169 | 0.030 | 3.10e-05 | 1.343 | 1.30e-05 |
| 15 | 0.7440 | 0.3349 | 0.8700 | 0.954 | 0.156 | 0.029 | 0.00e+00 | 1.065 | 4.83e-07 |

The validation minimum is at epoch 12; epochs 13-15 give 0.3332 / 0.3345 /
0.3349, i.e. +0.0026 total drift, and patience fires. Optimizer health is good
throughout: gradient norms 1.1–3.7 (never clipped to zero, never exploding) and
parameter-update ratios decaying smoothly from 1.5e-04 to 4.8e-07 as the cosine
anneals. Evaluation loss is *below* training loss for most of the run, so there
is no overfitting — the original "still descending at 8 epochs" reading was an
artifact of a 200-step schedule whose LR was still ramping.

The plain baseline behaves the same way at its own budget: best validation loss
0.2346 at epoch 7 of a 16-epoch cosine, early-stopped at epoch 10.

## 3. Learning-rate selection (validation only)

| system | lr | best val loss | best choice acc | outcome |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| VSS | 3e-4 | **0.3323** | **0.870** | selected |
| VSS | 1e-3 | 0.3629 | 0.865 | overfits after 275 steps (eval rising while train falls) |
| plain | 3e-4 | **0.2346** | **0.8675** | selected |
| plain | 1e-3 | 0.9923 | 0.500 | clearly worse |

3e-4 is selected for **both** systems, so the choice cannot be accused of
favouring either. 1e-3 is rejected on validation for both.

> Validation *loss* is **not** comparable across systems — VSS's objective adds
> a calibration-head BCE and a soft-target ordinal term that the plain
> `_batch_loss` does not have. Only the per-task metrics are comparable, and
> both trainers score choice rows by argmax over the row's declared options plus
> ABSTAIN.

## 4. Fair comparison

| system | steps | passes | best choice acc | best noul acc | best score MAE | params | wall s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| VSS | 400 | 16 | **0.8700** | 0.9550 | 0.1562 | 11,164,483 | 2,953 |
| plain | 2,200 | 11 | **0.8675** | 0.9513 | 0.1543 | 10,509,134 | 4,056 |

**At matched data exposure the two systems are tied on all three task types**
(0.870 vs 0.868 choice, 0.955 vs 0.951 noul, 0.156 vs 0.154 score MAE — all
within seed noise). VSS reaches that point with **5.5× fewer optimizer steps** and
**1.37× less wall-clock time**, because each VSS step back-propagates all 8
questions of a state jointly.

At the *equal-step* budget the comparison inverts hard (VSS 0.870 vs plain
0.511), which is the same fact seen from the other side: a step is not a unit of
learning signal across architectures with different inputs per step.

## 5. Re-measured multi-question benchmark (clean split, fair protocol)

`synthetic_fair_results.json`, seed 1, converged checkpoints, plain
presentation-matched:

| Q | plain | VSS | C−B (95% CI) | VSS coverage | VSS answered acc | plain ECE | VSS ECE |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.9950 | 1.0000 | +0.0050 [+0.0000, +0.0150] | 1.000 | 1.0000 | 0.008 | 0.005 |
| 2 | 0.9975 | 1.0000 | +0.0025 [+0.0000, +0.0075] | 1.000 | 1.0000 | 0.005 | 0.003 |
| 4 | 0.9725 | 0.9712 | −0.0013 [−0.0063, +0.0037] | 0.996 | 0.9749 | 0.165 | 0.013 |
| 8 | 0.9287 | 0.8612 | −0.0675 [−0.0800, −0.0550] | 0.874 | **0.9857** | 0.148 | 0.050 |
| 16 | 0.9325 | 0.8981 | −0.0344 [−0.0431, −0.0250] | 0.917 | **0.9792** | 0.128 | 0.041 |
| 32 | 0.8116 | 0.7614 | −0.0502 [−0.0592, −0.0408] | 0.935 | 0.8141 | 0.167 | 0.175 |
| 50 | 0.7695 | 0.7004 | −0.0691 [−0.0768, −0.0604] | 0.940 | 0.7453 | 0.184 | 0.234 |

- **Q=1–4: a tie** (CIs touch or span zero). VSS is marginally ahead at Q=1-2.
- **Q=8–16: plain leads by 3.4–6.8 pts, but almost all of that is VSS's
  abstention.** VSS answers 98.6% correctly at 87.4% coverage; the head-line
  0.8612 charges it for the 12.6% it declines.
- **Q=32–50: plain leads by 5.0–6.9 pts and abstention is not the whole story**
  — VSS's own answered accuracy has fallen to 0.814/0.745. The synthetic pool
  admits rephrased surface forms (`_b/_c/_d`) beyond Q=16, and the per-pair plain
  baseline generalises to unseen rephasings better than VSS's shared encoder
  does at this budget.
- **Calibration inverts with Q**: VSS is far better calibrated at Q≤16
  (ECE 0.003–0.050 vs 0.005–0.165), plain is better at Q=32/50.

Latency (p50, Q=50): A sequential 1,573 ms, B batched 465 ms, C VSS 174 ms —
**9.0× vs sequential, 2.7× vs the *batched* classifier**. The efficiency
advantage survives, and it is the one claim from the first value test that was
never in doubt.

Interference (solo vs joint, paired sets): **+8.13 pts at Q=8** (266/800 paired
decisions flip) and **+6.09 pts at Q=32** (1,225/3,200 flip) — co-asking the
questions *helps* rather than hurting. Permutation agreement 0.955 (Q=8) /
0.988 (Q=32).

## 6. Selective prediction, compared properly

The earlier report compared VSS's selective answered-accuracy against the plain
classifier's always-on accuracy, which flatters whichever side abstains. Both
systems were therefore re-scored on one risk-coverage curve (synthetic, Q=8,
1,600 questions, each question ranked by its own confidence):

| coverage | plain accuracy | VSS accuracy |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.00 | 0.9306 | 0.8644 |
| 0.95 | 0.9276 | 0.9099 |
| 0.90 | 0.9236 | 0.9604 |
| 0.85 | 0.9199 | **0.9919** |
| 0.80 | 0.9187 | **0.9961** |
| 0.70 | 0.9598 | 1.0000 |
| 0.60 | 0.9812 | 1.0000 |
| 0.50 | 0.9962 | 1.0000 |

**VSS dominates plain at every coverage ≤ 0.90.** Plain's confidence is close to
uninformative — dropping its least-confident 10% actually *lowers* accuracy
(0.9306 → 0.9236) — while VSS's is strongly informative (0.8644 → 0.9961 by
dropping 20%). At VSS's shipped gate (coverage 0.874) it delivers 0.9893 answered
accuracy against plain's 0.9306 always-on.

This is the strongest surviving argument for the architecture, and it is
**opposite** to what the starved runs suggested (where the gate looked
uninformative because the model behind it had barely trained).

## 7. Limitations — read before acting on this

1. **One seed.** Every number above is seed 1. No seed CI, no variance estimate.
   The task asked for 3 (5 if practical); the box was running at ~50% external
   load for the whole study and a VSS epoch cost ~5.5 min, so 3 seeds × 4 arms was
   not affordable. Differences below ~2 pts must not be treated as real.
2. **Synthetic only.** CLINC150 and Banking77 were **not** re-trained or
   re-benchmarked. Their splits are verified clean, but audit finding 3 (stable
   RoPE positions taken from example 0's state length) is **live on both** —
   state lengths are 7-24 and 10-30 tokens there versus a uniform 32 on
   synthetic. Their previously reported order-invariance and interference
   numbers are therefore known-suspect and are withdrawn until re-run. The
   question "how much did real-data performance change?" is **not answered**.
3. **Absolute timings are contended.** The machine ran at ~50% CPU from an
   unrelated process, inflating per-step cost ~4× versus the earlier idle runs.
   Within-cell latency ratios are measured back-to-back and remain valid;
   absolute milliseconds do not.
4. **Validation loss is not cross-system comparable** (§3).
5. **The rephrasing confound at Q>16** (§5) is a property of the synthetic
   generator, not of either architecture; a synthetic pool without surface-form
   variants would be needed to separate "VSS degrades with Q" from "VSS
   generalises across surface forms worse".
6. **Abstention threshold was not re-tuned.** VSS uses the shipped 0.55, chosen
   before this study. §6 shows the curve, so this is a missed opportunity rather
   than a confound, but no threshold was selected on test.

## 8. What this does and does not license

**Licensed:**

- The multi-question value test's central conclusion is **withdrawn**. "Plain
  beats VSS in 35/35 cells" was measured on a split where 80% of the test states
  were training states, against a VSS run that spent 75% of a 200-step schedule
  in warmup, with an 8× step-budget advantage handed to the baseline.
- VSS reaches a genuine convergence point and does not over-train on synthetic.
- VSS's multi-question efficiency claim survives (2.7× vs *batched* plain).
- VSS's selective prediction is demonstrably useful where plain's is not.

**Not licensed:**

- Any claim about real data. Nothing here re-measures CLINC150 or Banking77.
- Any scaling decision. This is one seed, one dataset, one budget.
- A general statement that "VSS ≥ plain": the tie is at this budget on this
  data, and plain wins Q≥32 outright.

## 9. Smallest next experiments, in priority order

1. **Re-run CLINC150 and Banking77** with the audit fixes and matched
   presentations (VSS 1 step ≈ 8 question-presentations). Everything real-data
   currently rests on known-suspect numbers. *Highest value by far.*
2. **3 seeds** on synthetic for the two converged configurations, to put a CI on
   the 0.870 / 0.8675 tie — if the gap is ~0 ± 0.01 the architectures are
   equivalent on this task and the choice should be made on latency and
   selectivity alone.
3. **A synthetic pool without surface-form rephasings**, to determine whether
   VSS's Q≥32 deficit is architectural or a generalisation artifact.
4. **Re-tune the abstention threshold on the calibration split** (never test)
   and re-measure whether the gate's operating point should move.
